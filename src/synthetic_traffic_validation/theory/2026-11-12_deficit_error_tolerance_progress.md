## Model and notation

Let $\mathcal C=\{c_i\mid i=1,\ldots,N\}$ be the cell set. Consecutive scheduling cycles are indexed by $j=1,2,\ldots$, and slots within a cycle by $t=1,\ldots,T$. The beam set is $\mathcal B$.

The nonnegative quantity $Z_i^j$ is the virtual service deficit of cell $c_i$ at the start of cycle $j$. The quantities $D_{i,j}$ and $S_{i,j}$ are the true cycle demand and realized allocated service capacity, respectively. All three quantities are measured in bits. Define $[z]^+=\max\{z,0\}$ and update

$$
Z_i^{j+1}=[Z_i^j+D_{i,j}-S_{i,j}]^+.
$$

The vector $\mathbf Z^j$ collects these deficits. The information sigma algebra $\mathcal F_j$ contains information available at cycle start. The selected plan $\mathbf u_j$ is measurable with respect to $\mathcal F_j$ and belongs to the feasible plan set $\mathcal U_j$.

For a candidate plan $\mathbf u$, let $m_{i,j}(\mathbf u)$ be its nominal service to cell $c_i$, in bits. The score is

$$
F_j(\mathbf u)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_i^j m_{i,j}(\mathbf u).
$$

With fixed link resources and additive edge service, $w_{j,t}^{b,i}$ is the nominal service in bits from choosing beam $b$ to serve cell $c_i$ in slot $t$. The matching input is

$$
\widetilde w_{j,t}^{b,i}=Z_i^j w_{j,t}^{b,i}.
$$

The deficit is frozen while planning the cycle. The weighted score has units of $\mathrm{bit}^2$; it is not a delivered traffic volume. These deficit weights differ from the normalized urgency coefficients in the existing experiments.

The current data interpretation separates demand and service information. Demand is generated by superposed continuous Pareto ON/OFF sources, and ARFIMA predicts the next cycle's aggregate demand. Channel service means and standard deviations are supplied by the experiment model; future service realizations are generated afterwards and are unavailable to the planner. A forecast is not automatically the true conditional mean.

## Reported relaxation of the service assumption

The original requirement that nominal service be a conditional service lower bound is relaxed to allow bounded overestimation for the selected plan:

$$
\mathbb E[S_{i,j}\mid\mathcal F_j]
\ge m_{i,j}(\mathbf u_j)-b_i^j,
\qquad 0\le b_i^j\le\rho_{\mathrm{model}}.
$$

Here $b_i^j$ is a service overestimation allowance, not a bandwidth variable. The deterministic nonnegative constant $\rho_{\mathrm{model}}$ is measured in bits.

## Reported relaxation of optimization accuracy

For deterministic constants $C\ge0$ and $\rho_{\mathrm{opt}}\ge0$, the selected feasible plan satisfies

$$
F_j(\mathbf u_j)
\ge \max_{\mathbf u\in\mathcal U_j}F_j(\mathbf u)
-C-\rho_{\mathrm{opt}}\sum_i Z_i^j.
$$

The units of $C$ are $\mathrm{bit}^2$, and those of $\rho_{\mathrm{opt}}$ are bits. This is a bound for the same score, feasible set, and frozen deficit values; it does not follow merely from stopping an optimization routine.

## Retained assumptions

The initial deficit has finite quadratic expectation. Define

$$
L(\mathbf z)=\frac12\sum_i z_i^2,
\qquad L_0=\mathbb E[L(\mathbf Z^1)]<\infty.
$$

Retain the original uniform conditional second moment assumption with deterministic constant $C_2$. One sufficient version is

$$
\frac12\sum_i\mathbb E[D_{i,j}^2+S_{i,j}^2\mid\mathcal F_j]\le C_2.
$$

The quadratic drift actually involves $(D_{i,j}-S_{i,j})^2$. Nonnegativity gives a convenient bound using the separate squares. Subject to the needed existence and integrability of the conditional expectations, a uniform bound on the squared net increments can instead be used. This is a clarification of the original moment condition, not a newly claimed improvement.

Retain strict conditional nominal service slack $\varepsilon>0$: given $\mathcal F_j$, there exists a randomized comparison among admissible plans whose mean nominal service to each cell exceeds $\mathbb E[D_{i,j}\mid\mathcal F_j]$ by at least $\varepsilon$.

In the original finite plan setting, let $p_j(\mathbf u)$ be the comparison probabilities. Then

$$
p_j(\mathbf u)\ge0,\qquad
\sum_{\mathbf u\in\mathcal U_j}p_j(\mathbf u)=1,
$$

$$
\sum_{\mathbf u\in\mathcal U_j}p_j(\mathbf u)m_{i,j}(\mathbf u)
\ge\mathbb E[D_{i,j}\mid\mathcal F_j]+\varepsilon,
\qquad \forall c_i\in\mathcal C.
$$

The average is over plans at a fixed cycle conditional on current information. It is neither an average across cells nor the time average along an actual execution. A linear program over plan mixture probabilities can help check the original capacity assumption. A finite sample check does not establish a uniform conditional assumption for all relevant histories.

No cycle independence is introduced. The conditional slack assumption remains strong and has not been established for the existing traffic and load configurations. Extension to continuous resource plans requires its own admissible plan and comparison measure definitions.

## Reported drift and average deficit bounds

Define the remaining service margin

$$
\eta=\varepsilon-\rho_{\mathrm{opt}}-\rho_{\mathrm{model}}.
$$

When $\eta>0$, the reported drift inequality is

$$
\mathbb E[L(\mathbf Z^{j+1})-L(\mathbf Z^j)\mid\mathcal F_j]
\le C_2+C-\eta\sum_i Z_i^j.
$$

Its proof outline combines the original quadratic drift inequality, the selected plan's service overestimation allowance, the approximate score condition, and the unchanged conditional slack comparison. The two error terms each consume part of that slack.

For every positive integer $J$, summing the drift yields

$$
\frac1J\sum_{j=1}^{J}\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_i Z_i^j\right]
\le\frac{C_2+C}{\eta}+\frac{L_0}{\eta J}.
$$

Setting both error coefficients to zero recovers the original margin $\varepsilon$ and bound. This note preserves the reported extension and outline; it is not a new independently checked full proof document.

## Reported finite cycle consequence

Define cumulative positive net shortfall for cell $c_i$ by

$$
U_i(J)=\left[\sum_{j=1}^{J}D_{i,j}-\sum_{j=1}^{J}S_{i,j}\right]^+.
$$

The same drift argument gives

$$
\mathbb E\!\left[\sum_i U_i(J)\right]
\le\sqrt{2N\bigl(L_0+J(C_2+C)\bigr)}.
$$

Consequently the expected cumulative net shortfall divided by the number of cycles tends to zero. The positive part is taken after summing over cycles. This allows cross cycle compensation and does not bound the sum of individual cycle positive shortfalls.

## What is and is not established

- The statements concern long term virtual deficit and cumulative service accounting.
- They do not guarantee demand satisfaction in every cycle, a deadline, a chance constraint, or optimal raw throughput.
- Allocated service capacity may exceed a cycle's demand. It must not silently be reinterpreted as useful delivered traffic or a physical queue departure.
- Precise per slot TU matching gives zero score optimization error only in the stated fixed resource, additive, uncoupled model, using the frozen deficit weights and an integral optimal matching.
- Existing normalized urgency experiments do not validate this deficit policy. Current implementations have not acquired a persistent cross cycle service deficit through this discussion.
- Interval sampled cycle comparisons and continuous operational trajectories cannot be interchanged to argue dynamic stability.
- Reported self checks do not constitute independent review or Lean verification.

## Possible later use in resource decomposition

Generalized Benders decomposition could certify the score optimization error through feasible lower bounds and valid upper bounds for the same frozen problem. It cannot establish the service model bias bound. Standard Benders cuts do not preserve a pure TU master problem automatically.

Demand forecasting error is not directly the service model allowance $\rho_{\mathrm{model}}$. Any effect of forecasting on the score or the selected decision must be analyzed separately.

The potentially substantive follow up work identified in the supplied summary is to derive a computable capacity margin $\varepsilon$, a justified service overestimation bound $\rho_{\mathrm{model}}$, and a certified optimization error bound from a concrete model and algorithm. These are candidate research tasks, not adopted requirements or completed results.
